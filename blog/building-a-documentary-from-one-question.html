How to Build a Documentary Starting With One Question

A strong documentary does not begin with a pile of dramatic footage. It begins with a specific question, a research plan and a promise to the viewer that the story will be worth their time.

By Mahesh Ishana · Educational article · More articles

A strong documentary does not begin with a pile of dramatic footage. It begins with a specific question, a research plan and a promise to the viewer that the story will be worth their time.

Narrow the subject

“Social media is changing society” is too broad for one video. A more useful starting point asks how a specific feature, incentive or behaviour affects a defined group in a particular context.

Write the central question

Phrase the investigation as something evidence can help answer. The question should create curiosity without assuming the conclusion in advance.

Make a source map

List official documents, research papers, expert explanations, credible reporting and first-hand perspectives that could illuminate the topic. Record links and dates as you go.

Build an evidence table

For each major claim, note the source, what it supports, its limitations and what still needs checking. This helps prevent a powerful line from outrunning the evidence.

Structure for clarity

A practical outline is: hook, context, central question, evidence, counterpoint, human impact and takeaway. Each section should move the investigation forward.

Keep production realistic

A focused face-camera setup, clear voice-over, relevant screenshots and a few well-chosen visuals can communicate more than a complicated edit. Prioritise accuracy and clarity over effects.
