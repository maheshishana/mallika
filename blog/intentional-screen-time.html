Why Endless Scrolling Feels Hard to Stop

Short videos and feeds make it easy to keep going. Instead of relying only on willpower, make your phone environment support the habits you actually want.

By Mahesh Ishana · Educational article · More articles

Short videos and feeds make it easy to keep going. Instead of relying only on willpower, make your phone environment support the habits you actually want.

Small decisions add up

A quick check can turn into a long session when one post leads to another. Notifications, autoplay and an always-available feed reduce the number of natural stopping points.

Notice your triggers

For a few days, notice when you reach for your phone: boredom, stress, waiting, loneliness or avoiding a difficult task. The goal is not to judge yourself; it is to spot the pattern.

Create friction for distracting apps

Turn off non-essential notifications, move social apps off the home screen, log out of accounts you open automatically, and keep your phone away during focused work. Small barriers can interrupt autopilot.

Choose a replacement, not just a restriction

Decide what you will do instead: take a short walk, stretch, call a friend, read a few pages or work for ten minutes on one specific task. A clear alternative is easier to follow than a vague promise to stop scrolling.

Use a realistic reset

Try one phone-free block each day, such as the first 20 minutes after waking or a focused work session. Review what helps and adjust the plan instead of treating one slip as failure.

Make the feed serve you

Unfollow accounts that leave you feeling worse, save useful content intentionally and set a reason before opening an app. The aim is not to avoid technology altogether, but to use it with more choice.
