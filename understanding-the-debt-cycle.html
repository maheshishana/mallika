Understanding the Debt Cycle: Questions to Ask Before Borrowing

Borrowing can solve an immediate problem, but repayments can become difficult when the full cost and monthly budget are ignored. Here are practical questions to ask before taking credit.

By Mahesh Ishana · Educational article · More articles

Borrowing can solve an immediate problem, but repayments can become difficult when the full cost and monthly budget are ignored. Here are practical questions to ask before taking credit.

Why borrowing can become a cycle

Debt becomes harder to manage when a new loan or credit line is used to cover an older repayment. The short-term pressure may feel smaller, while the total amount owed and the number of due dates can grow.

Look beyond the advertised amount

Before accepting credit, identify the total repayment, interest, processing fees, late fees, repayment schedule and consequences of missing a payment. Read the agreement rather than relying only on a banner or phone conversation.

Check the monthly reality

List essential expenses first: housing, food, utilities, transport and family needs. Then compare existing repayments with dependable take-home income. If a repayment only works in a perfect month, it may be too risky.

Pause before using one debt to pay another

Taking fresh credit to pay an old balance can increase costs and delay the underlying problem. Before doing so, understand all charges and consider contacting the lender to discuss available options. Do not ignore formal notices or rely on verbal promises alone.

Build a small decision checklist

Ask: Is this need urgent? Can I delay the purchase? What is the total repayment? What happens if my income falls? Have I compared alternatives? Can I afford the payment without missing essentials?

A note on financial decisions

This article is general education, not personal financial or legal advice. Terms vary by lender and product. Check official documents and consult a qualified adviser when needed.
