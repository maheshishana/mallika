Credit Cards: Five Things to Understand Before You Swipe

A credit card is a payment tool, not extra income. Understanding the statement, due date and interest rules can help you avoid expensive surprises.

By Mahesh Ishana · Educational article · More articles

A credit card is a payment tool, not extra income. Understanding the statement, due date and interest rules can help you avoid expensive surprises.

The statement balance matters

Understand the total amount billed, the minimum amount due and the payment due date. Paying only the minimum may leave a balance that attracts interest, depending on the card terms.

A minimum payment is not a debt-free plan

The minimum due is the amount required to avoid being treated as late under the issuer’s terms; it does not mean the rest of the balance has been cleared. Check your statement for interest and fees.

Know the interest-free period conditions

Interest-free benefits typically depend on the card terms and whether previous balances are paid in full. Cash advances and certain transactions may be treated differently.

Check fees and terms

Review annual fees, late-payment charges, cash advance fees, foreign currency charges and other applicable costs. Read the issuer’s official terms because details vary by card.

Use a repayment routine

Set reminders before the due date, review transactions regularly and spend only what your budget can repay. If you are already struggling, contact the card issuer through its official channels to understand your options.

Important note

This is general educational information. Card terms vary by issuer and can change. Refer to your latest statement and official card agreement.
